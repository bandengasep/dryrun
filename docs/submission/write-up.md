# DryRun — rehearse the interview you're about to have

## Problem

Interview prep is generic; the interview you're about to have is specific. Question banks don't know what this job description demands or what your resume is silent on, and a chatbot rehearsing you will invent both the questions and the praise, anchored to no line of either document. LinkedIn Premium now ships AI Role Play practice — the demand is mainstream; DryRun's bet is narrower: prep you can audit. Paste a JD and a resume; DryRun diffs them into evidenced gaps, compiles the interview those gaps predict, runs it live against an AI interviewer, and debriefs you by quoting your own answers back. Success criteria — grounding, consistency, precision/recall, cost, latency, a zero-shot baseline — were locked 26 Jul, before building (`docs/spec-pivot-2026-07-26.md`).

## Approach

The design law is one chain of receipts, three links deep. Every gap cites a JD span and a resume span, enforced as literal slices — `sourceText.slice(start, end) === text`, checked in code. Every interview question carries the `gapId` of the gap that produced it; a question citing a nonexistent gap throws at a guard, never renders. Every debrief claim anchors to a verbatim transcript quote, re-located mechanically with the same span locator; quotes that fail validation demote to a visible `dropped[]` count — shown, never silently discarded. Nothing is graded: the debrief says what an answer covered and missed, in the candidate's own words. No scores anywhere.

Alternatives we named and rejected: the assess-and-advise coach pattern — AI-asserted judgment behind a "may make mistakes" disclaimer — replaced with mechanical validation; an agent framework to fix latency (doc-verified 31 Jul: orchestration layers don't cut inference time, where our seconds go); the Vercel AI SDK (the OpenAI SDK's strict structured outputs were the proven receipts path); and ambient database storage — session state is client-held, routes are stateless, and the single server write is the explicit "Save & share" button. Receipts-critical structured calls run on OpenAI strict structured output; the conversational interviewer lane runs on Agnes AI with labeled OpenAI failover.

## Evidence

Every locked criterion is reported — met, missed, or restated; none silently dropped. Full table in the README; raw runs in `evals/results/`.

Grounding: 29/29 questions (100%) cited a real gap before the guard, across five pairs — re-verified 30/30 on the adopted engine; the guard makes displayed grounding 100% by construction. Consistency: mean pairwise Jaccard 0.794/0.900 against ≥0.6, 0.881/0.770 on the adopted engine. Latency, after a gated 31-Jul program: compile 16.6–19.1s fixture and 40–45s real Venture JD in production, plan 9.4–11.0s, debrief 6.9s — met by adopting a newer model only after it passed the same gates. The faster path we refused: lowering reasoning effort cut latency 4× but collapsed requirement extraction — speed bought by reading less of the JD. First-turn token stays missed at 6.9–9.2s, gateway-bound on the partner lane, restated.

The zero-shot baseline did not show what we expected: asked politely to cite, gpt-5-mini was 1.03% uncited (28 Jul) and 0.0% on the audited re-run — 100/100 quotes verbatim. Our claim was never that models can't quote. It is that the baseline's citedness is a habit with no enforcement — its one 28-Jul fabrication would have shipped undetected — and its quotes are unanchored strings a reader cannot check; verifying its 0% required exactly the locator machinery DryRun ships. Provider exhibit: on plan compile, Agnes ran 2.40× faster than OpenAI at receipts parity — 13.2s vs 31.6s mean, grounding 12/12 and STAR compliance 100% on both.

## Constraints

Cost: not fully captured — we report that rather than invent a number — cost threading in the eval suites nulls out, and Agnes publishes no per-token price, so we report token counts (≈$0.035/compile derivable at posted rates, labeled derived). Latency: three of four targets met through the gated model swap; the fourth restated with its measurement. Reliability: labeled per-request failover on the interviewer lane; guards that throw rather than display an ungrounded receipt; SSE with 10-second heartbeats on stateless routes. Safety and privacy: production Langfuse traces mask document bodies — JD, resume, and answers are PII, and observability must not quietly violate "nothing stored unless you save"; nothing persists server-side except the explicit write-once "Save & share" row; screenshot ingestion lands as an editable draft — vision is never the source of record.

## Honesty & Trajectory

Gap precision/recall against gold: not measured. The human adjudicator fell ill, and we declined AI adjudication twice on circularity — one delegated pass was reverted after producing zero missed-requirements on all five pairs, a fake recall of 1.0. Gold stays human-adjudicated or unmeasured, never synthetic; the protocol, review sheets, and taxonomy validator are committed and CI-green; adjudication is the first post-deadline task. The video-answer lane was no-go'd at its pre-committed gate and re-rejected after the deadline extension — the gate working, not a feature dying; its substrate ships dormant (14 passing timeline tests) and the debrief loses only timestamps. Mic dictation shipped behind a flag, unevaluated: speech lands as an editable draft, and review-before-send keeps quote validation honest. Our own evals surfaced a real limitation: the engine over-generates — 168 gaps collapse to 89 distinct JD requirements — so the plan page caps the display behind a show-all expander; deduplication is the named next step. The SQL challenge harness stays in-repo, dormant and CI-green: the next tier, parked, not abandoned.

Run it live at dryrun-web-pi.vercel.app or locally: `pnpm install && pnpm --filter web dev` (README). Runtime needs OpenAI (required), Agnes (labeled failover), Supabase (explicit save only). Judges ask whether they'd interview this applicant; our answer is the product's own move: don't take the claim — check the receipts.
