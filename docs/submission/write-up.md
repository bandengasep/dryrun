# DryRun — rehearse the interview you're about to have

## Problem

Interview prep is generic; the interview you are about to have is specific. Question banks don't know what this job description demands or what your resume is silent on, and a chatbot asked to rehearse you will invent both the questions and the praise, anchored to no line of either document. LinkedIn Premium's launch of AI Role Play interview practice shows this demand is now mainstream. DryRun's bet is narrower than the mainstream pattern: prep you can audit. Paste a JD and a resume; DryRun diffs them into evidenced gaps, compiles the interview those gaps predict, runs it live against an AI interviewer, and debriefs you by quoting your own answers back. Success criteria — grounding, consistency, precision/recall, cost, latency, a zero-shot baseline — were locked on 26 Jul, before building (`docs/spec-pivot-2026-07-26.md`).

## Approach

The design law is one chain of receipts, three links deep. Every gap cites a JD span and a resume span, enforced as literal slices — `sourceText.slice(start, end) === text`, checked in code. Every interview question carries the `gapId` of the gap that produced it; a question citing a nonexistent gap throws at a guard, never renders. Every debrief claim anchors to a verbatim transcript quote, re-located mechanically with the same span locator; quotes that fail validation are demoted to a visible `dropped[]` count — shown, never silently discarded. And nothing is graded: the debrief says what an answer covered and missed, in the candidate's own words. No scores anywhere.

Alternatives we named and rejected: the assess-and-advise coach pattern — AI-asserted strengths and weaknesses behind a "may make mistakes" disclaimer — replaced with mechanical validation instead of asserted judgment; migrating to an agent framework to fix latency (doc-verified 31 Jul: orchestration layers don't cut inference time, which is where our seconds go); the Vercel AI SDK (27 Jul — the OpenAI SDK's strict structured outputs were the proven receipts path); and ambient database storage — session state is client-held and routes are stateless; the single server write is the explicit "Save & share" button. Receipts-critical structured calls run on OpenAI strict structured output; the conversational interviewer lane runs on Agnes AI with labeled per-request OpenAI failover.

## Evidence

Every locked criterion is reported — met, missed, or restated; none silently dropped. Full table in the README; raw runs in `evals/results/`.

Grounding: 29/29 questions (100%) cited a real gap before the guard, across five JD-resume pairs; the guard makes the displayed rate 100% by construction. Consistency: mean pairwise Jaccard 0.794 (pair-01, 20 runs) and 0.900 (pair-04) against ≥0.6. Debrief latency: 22.7s against ≤45s. If the Saturday program moves nothing, those rows stand as restated misses with measurements — compile 99.65s fixture / 146.3s real JD vs ≤60s, plan 33.4s vs ≤20s, first token ~6.2s vs ≤3s — each with a named, untried lever.

The zero-shot baseline did not show what we expected, and we say so: asked politely to cite, gpt-5-mini was 1.03% uncited (28 Jul) and 0.0% on the audited re-run — 100/100 quotes verbatim. Our claim was never that models can't quote. It is that the baseline's citedness is a habit with no enforcement — its one 28-Jul fabrication would have shipped undetected — and its quotes are unanchored strings a reader cannot check; verifying its 0% required exactly the locator machinery DryRun ships. The claim is enforcement plus verifiability, not model incapacity. Provider exhibit: on plan compile, Agnes ran 2.40× faster than OpenAI at receipts parity — 13.2s vs 31.6s mean, grounding 12/12 and STAR compliance 100% on both.

## Constraints

Cost: not fully captured, and we report that rather than invent a number — cost threading in the eval suites nulls out, and Agnes publishes no per-token price, so we report token counts. Latency: ⟦LATENCY-OUTCOME⟧ — misses restated with measurements above. Reliability: per-request provider failover on the interviewer lane, labeled in the UI; guards that throw rather than display an ungrounded receipt; SSE streaming with 10-second heartbeats on stateless serverless routes. Safety and privacy: production Langfuse traces mask document bodies — JD, resume, and answers are PII, and observability must not quietly violate "nothing stored unless you save"; nothing persists server-side except the explicit write-once "Save & share" row; screenshot ingestion lands as an editable draft the user confirms — vision is never the source of record.

## Honesty & Trajectory

Gap precision/recall against gold: not measured. The human adjudicator fell ill, and we declined AI adjudication twice on circularity — one delegated pass was reverted after it produced zero missed-requirements on all five pairs, a fake recall of 1.0. Gold stays human-adjudicated or unmeasured, never synthetic; the protocol, review sheets, and taxonomy validator are committed and CI-green, and adjudication is the first post-deadline task. The video-answer lane was no-go'd at its pre-committed gate, called early on evidence, and re-rejected after the deadline extension — the gate working, not a feature dying; its substrate ships dormant with 14 passing timeline tests, and the debrief loses only timestamps. Mic dictation shipped behind a flag as an unevaluated input convenience: speech lands as an editable draft, and review-before-send keeps quote validation honest. Our own evals surfaced a real limitation: the engine over-generates — 168 gaps collapse to 89 distinct JD requirements — so the plan page caps the display with a show-all expander as the honest interim, and deduplication is the named next step. The SQL challenge harness stays in-repo, dormant and CI-green: the next tier, parked, not abandoned. Judges ask whether they would interview this applicant. Our answer is the product's own move: don't take the claim — check the receipts.
